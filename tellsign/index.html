The grammar of a scam

Five tells, in almost every case

The surface changes constantly — a bank, a courier, a tax office, a recruiter, a romance. The underlying structure barely changes at all. Teach the structure and the surface stops mattering.

Tell 01

Manufactured time pressure

A deadline exists to stop you checking. Real institutions let you call back; they do not lose your money because you took an hour.

"within 30 minutes" · "final notice" · "your account will be suspended today"

Tell 02

The contact route is supplied

The link and the phone number are in the message. That is the whole trick: you are being handed the route to the people running it.

Any number, link or QR code inside the message itself

Tell 03

Isolation

You are told not to tell anyone — framed as security, a confidential investigation, or not troubling your family. Secrecy protects them, never you.

"do not discuss this case" · "we are testing internal staff"

Tell 04

An irreversible payment

Bank transfer, cryptocurrency, gift card codes, cash couriers. All chosen for one property: once sent, the money cannot be recalled.

"move it to a safe account" · "pay the fee in vouchers"

Tell 05

A near-miss identity

A domain, sender ID or caller ID that is close but not exact. All three can be forged, so what looks right proves nothing.

hsbc-secure-verify.com · rnicrosoft.com · a spoofed caller ID

If it is happening right now

Stop before you pay

This advice is free and does not require us. If you only ever read one part of this site, read this part.

  1. Stop the conversation. Hang up, close the chat, do not reply. Nothing genuine is lost by pausing. Pressure to stay on the line is itself the warning.
  2. Do not use their details. Find the number yourself — on your bank card, your statement, or by typing the official address into the browser. Never the number in the message.
  3. Use a different device if you can. If you are unsure whether a call really ended, call from another phone or wait before dialling.
  4. Tell one person. Say it out loud to somebody you trust. Isolation is the mechanism; breaking it usually breaks the scam.
  5. If money has moved, call your bank immediately and report it to your national fraud reporting service. Speed materially affects what can be recovered.
